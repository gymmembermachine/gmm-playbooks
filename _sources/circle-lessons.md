## **Overview**

The March Madness Team Challenge is a bracket-style, **team-based** competition designed to:

- Boost member engagement + excitement + "owner cares" feelings
- Increase referrals
- Boost session attendance
- Generate 5-star reviews
- Collect video testimonials
- Strengthen community
- Drive new member growth via referrals, reactivations, and organic posts
- Make a bit of extra cash from current members
- Generate buzz in and outside of the gym

Teams will compete weekly based on points earned for things like referrals, reviews, video testimonials, and session attendance.

---

## **Entry Fee & Prize Structure**

### **Entry Fee [Current Members]**

Charge $20–$50 per participant (waived if they refer a new member prior to start of program*)

*Important for the challenge - referral "points" (we'll go over those in a bit) earned *before *the start of the program will count towards the team's week one matchup.

---

### **Free Entry Incentive (Pre-Launch Only)**

If a member refers someone BEFORE the official launch date:

- Their entry fee is waived
- The referred participant's entry fee is waived (have to pay for membership or FEO that covers duration of program)
- The team still receives referral points for Week 1

Goal = early referral momentum and new signups during promo period

---

## **Team Structure**

### **Team Size**

- 4–6 members per team (4 feels like sweet spot but you can adjust this)
- Referred participants get added to their referrer's team - so team gets points for the referral but also the ability to gain the additional points of the referred member for other "desired behaviors" e.g. reviews, attendance, etc.

### **How to Form Teams**

You may:

- Assign teams randomly (recommended) - tools like [this one ](https://www.randomlists.com/team-generator)allow you to generate teams quickly once you have your participant list locked down
- Group by session time (downsides here = imbalanced team size, lack of connection to members outside session, limited capacity for referred clients)
- Draw names from a hat (you could make this an event like Selection Sunday at a local bar if you wanted to get fancy/go a little higher budget)
- Allow members to choose (less recommended but has its upsides - could be cliquey, lead to people not having teams, smaller teams/less friends can feel bad, etc. but could also create peer pressure to get internal signups)

**Recommendation:** Random assignment prevents politics and increases cross-session friendshipping

---

## **Bracket Format**

### **Choose One:**

- 16-team bracket (smaller gyms)
- 32-team bracket (larger gyms)

*Again, base your bracket number on teams of about 4*

Sample bracket designs if you'd like to print a fancy poster can be found [here (16-team & 32-team options)](https://www.canva.com/design/DAHB9_HefJs/1j6uUqVfcQBTU6Z029z9oQ/view)

### **Weekly Advancement**

- Teams compete weekly
- Winners advance based on total points earned
- Losing teams move into a consolation bracket (recommended)

### **Consolation Bracket**

- Keeps eliminated teams engaged
- Maintains effort throughout the challenge
- Option to award a separate prize
- Option to have consolation winner face main bracket winner

**Recommended:** Use a consolation bracket to prevent early disengagement (minimum two weeks until "elimination" instead of one)

**Bracket & Spreadsheet tracker link [click to make a copy]**

---

## **4. Prize Guidelines**

### Grand Prize

Perceived Grand Prize value should be between 2-4x entry fee, but the more exciting you make it the easier it'll be to get folks to sign up. Higher margin, high perceived value items/one-time services are ideal here.

Be strategic - the concept itself will be motivating to some but the more enticing you can make the prize pool the more engagement you'll get from members.

Examples:

- Swag bundle (shirts, hoodie, hats)
- Swag + supplements
- Cash prize
- JV donations
- Hybrid (cash + gear)

**Important if doing JV/cash prize as part or all of prize pool**: The prize is awarded to a team. Ensure total perceived value feels meaningful when split.

### The "Everyone" Pool to Drive Participation

We recommend adding either a team or individual point threshold that allows everyone who hits a given point threshold to win a free gym-branded March Madness shirt at the end of the program.

Bonus points if you create a consolation drawing for everyone in this bucket (something like a $50 or $100 gym gift card for swag/supps/etc. could work well).

E.g. everyone who gets over X points gets the March Madness shirt and is put into the drawing for the consolation prize. The ONE person drawn gets the additional $50 or $100 gift card.

### Other Engagement-Boosting Options

**Tournament MVP: **highest individual points earned gets an additional higher level prize (e.g. free month membership)

---

## **5. Points System**

Teams earn weekly points for behaviors that grow your gym.

### **Core Point Categories**

Referrals

Session attendance

5-star reviews (Google, Facebook, etc.)

Video testimonials

Optional: Social check-ins or other behaviors (e.g. coach check-ins or other things you'd like members to do) *PROCEED WITH CAUTION HERE - complexity is a killer at worst and can be a major time suck *

---

### **Important: Weight Referrals Heavily**

Referrals should carry the highest point value.

The teams that bring in the most new people should have the greatest competitive advantage.

Expect some teams to grow beyond 6 members. That is intentional and part of the magic.

---

### **Social Check-Ins (Optional)**

You may award points for posts that tag the gym's FB/IG pages. Can be nice but only include this if you have staff bandwidth to track it consistently. Manual tracking increases complexity.

---

### **"Weekly Bonus" (Optional move to drop administrative load) **

We recommend that referrals & attendance serve as a constant "point getting" mechanism throughout the challenge, but you can also create a "weekly bonus" that incentives specific behaviors *so that you only have to track the numbers during that week. *

For example:

Week 1 (round of 32) Bonus: Google Reviews

Week 2 (round of 16) Bonus: Video Testimonials

Week 3 (round of 8) Bonus: Nextdoor (or Yelp, or FB, or "choose a platform" review)

Week 4 (final four) Bonus: Whiteboard success & gym swag in public tagged photo pics

Week 5 (championship) Bonus: Everything's a bonus (last chance to do it all)

## **7. Weekly Execution Flow**

Each week:

1. Tally total team points
2. Announce winners publicly
3. Update bracket standings
4. Share leaderboard
5. Celebrate referral leaders

Visibility drives competition.

Competition drives action.

Make it public and consistent.

The more fun & hype you have, the more fun (and engaged) your members will have with it.

---

## **8. Participation Shirt Incentive [Recommended]**

Any team that hits a defined point threshold earns:

A limited-edition March Madness shirt.

This applies even if they lose in the bracket.

Purpose:

- Reduces disengagement
- Maintains effort
- Chance for folks to wear your swag in public

---

## **9. Handling Referrals During the Challenge**

When a team refers someone:

- They earn referral points
- The referred participant may:
- Join their team
- Enter free
- Contribute to future points

Teams may grow significantly… *that's what we want. *

---

## **10. Optional Prize Enhancements**

If desired, you may:

- Partner with local businesses [full transparency, this is a challenge based on your timelines and the fact that prizes would have be given to the group unless you do multiple one-off bonus raffles, though it *could *be an easy way to get your foot in the door with a "give first" opener - "we're running a fun March Madness thing in the gym and I'd love to buy a gift card to raffle off to members…"]
- Add gift cards or bundled services [gift cards, free upgrades, supp starter packs, BOGO on swag]
- This will vary location to location, but anything you can do to increase perceived value without increasing cash spend is a win.

---

## **11. What Success Looks Like**

If executed properly, you should expect:

- Referral spike before launch
- Increased session attendance [ya
y decreased churn]
- Surge in 5-star reviews [yay long term SEO juice & social proof]
- Big bump in referral activity during challenge
- Increased member engagement
- Stronger community culture
- New member growth

---

## **12. Your Responsibilities as Gym Owner**

To implement successfully, you'll need to:

- Form teams
- Select entry fee and prize
- Promote like hell internally (use GMM's resources but a lot of the juice will come from your member communication, creating a little peer pressure, a lot of excitement, etc.)
- Track points weekly
- Update standings publicly
- Promote weekly
- Celebrate progress consistently

Energy from YOU and your team will determine the energy you get from your members.

**AUDIENCE: Non-members (totally fine to go to never-been & past members here)**

This is an LBO (low barrier offer) variation you can use as a "micro-commitment" to get folks off your list and into the gym before summer.

It's written as a 21-day "jumpstart" for $99, and while I don't love these types of offers for ads plays (though if you love this idea and want to ask our squad about, we have ad copy for ya), they *can *be useful for non-member & past-member reactivations.

**THE BIG IDEA? **

Turn those LBOs into members!

Ideally rock a Netflix close with these folks (talk membership options at consult/SPS, set them up for the discounted 21-days that will autorenew into a membership unless they decide to cancel anytime in the trial period.

Alternatively, you can do the "second sale" method that requires another conversation.

Two "primary" options here:

**Fast action offer at "momentum" point of trial**. Discount of some kind OR apply the $99 towards future membership (if you charge enough could just discount the first month e.g. $399 → $300 month 1, then $399 afterwards) or drip it across a longer time period if you do long term agreements.

**End-of-program check-in w/ second sale convo**. Essentially the same as a normal sales process with a bit more "context" about the client's attendance, goals, enjoyment if they've been attending, etc.

**OTHER CONSIDERATIONS **

*Referrals *

This *can *be an opportunity for a "soft" referral ask from members.

"Hey friends, FYI we're launching a promo this month celebrating "Springing Into Fitness" for new members. They'll get to try the program for 21 days for $99, but we're only opening a handful of spots. If you've got someone you've been telling to get to the gym but needed a good excuse… this is probably it!

Just have them CTA asap before we hit the email list about it so we can make sure we get them in.

THANK YOUS & SIGNOFF

These are nice-to-haves, but absolutely not must-haves to make this work. If you don't have those things, either let our squad know (if we're running it for you), remove the bullets that mention that in the copy [[G-doc with those aspects highlighted for your convenience here](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1U0aw5aDPLq1WMhhSrZ950OVXzNqZKGApzyjKLE5ompI/edit)], or use this as an excuse to create your community or start sending weekly tips to your clients to see how they respond. We'll make this simple - if your churn's under 5% — skip the fancy funnels, crazy discounts, and "buy my Black Friday that doesn't work" goo-roo nonsense.

Your most powerful move here is typically going to be a reactivation (with "never been" and "used to be" clients). Details on that below, but first, let's talk about timelines & ads…

### RE: Timeline

Black Friday has shifted earlier and earlier in the month these days. If you need or want to do something earlier in the month it can absolutely be a viable strategy.

### RE: Ads

There are essentially 3 situations you'll probably find yourself in:

1. **Have an offer that's currently working for you and you don't really care to change things?** KEEP RUNNING IT! Maaaybe talk to your CS team in Slack and see if the squad thinks adding Black Friday "wrappers" might keep things rolling for you, but otherwise, if something's working, keep doing it!

The most effective answer *tends *to be a "curiosity" Black Friday offer (e.g. an ad without mentioning specific discounts or parameters) but other options *can *work depending on you, your gym, and your market.
