The offer

Two ways to get started

Free A/B is an offer for group and semi-private gyms. You market a true free 30 days. At the end of the roadmap, you show two ways to get started side by side. A is the free 30 days. B is a legacy rate about 15% off that starts today. If they want the free 30, they get it.

Why it works: a true free 30 is an easy yes, so more people book and show up, and the "what does it cost?" objection goes away. B gives the people who already know they're in a reason to start today, so you still collect cash on day one.

  1. Market itRun ads and old-lead texts for a true free 30 days. G.R.: "Who else is marketing a true free 30 days in your area? Nobody."
  2. Book the roadmapOn the booking call and again in the roadmap, tell them you'll go over two ways to get started.
  3. Show A and BA: free 30 days, card on file, $0 today. B: the legacy rate, paid today and locked in. Then let them pick.
  4. Day 30Trialists roll into the membership at the regular rate unless they cancel before the date you gave them.
AFree 30 days
  • 30 days completely on the house. No catch and no bait and switch.
  • They "use and abuse" it and train like a member from day one.
  • Netflix close is optional. If you use it, card goes on file, the first invoice is $0, and you tell them the exact date of the first bill.
  • Miss the date and they roll into the full rate. The first gym running it does the trial at 2x a week and rolls it into a 2x a week membership.
  • No cash for about four weeks.

G.R.: "if they want to take you up on the free 30 day trial, by god you will give it to them."

BLegacy rate
  • Start today at about 15–16% off, locked in for as long as they're a member.
  • B breaks even with A around month six, so a B member saves money from month seven.
  • Cash on day one.
  • Name it for your stage: legacy, founding member, fast action, Thanksgiving special.
  • The B rate has to hit your profit goals even if it's the only thing you ever sell.
  • One gym's B extras: a 30-minute starting point session, a quick-start nutrition guide, a welcome box.

G.R.: "now you have a way to turn that into cash day one and put them into a membership."

The 30 days

Treat them like members on day one

Pratt's one-liner on converting free trials, as G.R. tells it: "they just treat people like members day one." Under a Netflix close the trial ends with a bill, not a sales call, so the selling happens in the experience.

Roadmap

Card on file, $0 first invoice, agreement dated to the first bill, waiver dated today. Book their first session before they leave. Don't let policies be the last thing they hear. Finish with the welcome gift.

Before session 1

Hand off their goals and injuries to the coach. G.R.'s "magic trick" is to have the trainer bring up something they shouldn't know about the client. It shows the handoff is tight and that you care.

After session 1

Ask for feedback right away: "How'd that feel intensity-wise? Do we need to turn things up or turn things down?" G.R. expects missing these early feedback conversations to be the biggest miss in most gyms.

Weeks 1–4

Act on the feedback and keep the sessions great. Watch attendance. If a trialist never shows, reframe and restart their trial date. If someone's showing up but on the fence, extending a week is fine.

Billing date

They roll into the membership. If someone forgot to cancel and gets grumpy, refund them, have a feedback conversation and try to save them. Fighting it gets you one-star reviews.

When it fits

Use it, or don't

Good fit

  • Group or semi-private training. G.R. sees it becoming the offer for a lot of the non-1-on-1 gym industry.
  • Your product sells better than your salespeople do. Let the product carry the sales load.
  • You need butts in seats now and have the room for them.
  • Prospects compare you to cheaper gyms. That objection mostly disappears once they see how good the food is.
  • Your B rate still hits your profit goals on its own.

Think twice

  • 1-on-1 training. Tougher to make the financials work. In a one-on-one training business, if folks are taking you up on longer-term trials, capacity is tight.
  • Capacity is tight. Consider running it as a small reactivation offer test, as opposed to a heavy, aggressive paid traffic offer.
  • If your current offer is crushing, don't do this. Keep running the play. It's working.
Mistakes

Where it breaks

  • Discounting B below your margin. G.R.: "the spot where this would break is if you discount off of rates that do not give you a margin."
  • Hiding the terms. Say the billing date out loud and put it in writing. A Netflix close needs heavy transparency.
  • Planning on 83%. Plan on 50–60% and be happy when you beat it.
  • Fighting refunds after an auto-charge. Just refund, then have the feedback conversation.
  • Skipping feedback after session one.
  • Ending the signup on policies. Finish with the welcome gift.
