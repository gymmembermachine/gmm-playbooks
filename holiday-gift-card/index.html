01 The Rundown

The holiday letter +
gift card / gift of fitness

This play is a great opportunity to drive both referrals and goodwill with your clients during the holidays.

The play, in 4 steps

  1. Letter from you to your members (hand sign each one, if you've got a big team let them sign them)
  2. Attach a nice gift card w/ dollar value & expiration date clearly stated for them to give as gifts. Ideally send w/ a nice gift card envelope that's easy to give to a friend or a family member. Include the policy (new clients only) nicely in the letter.
  3. Send or hand to clients in person. Ideally around Thanksgiving to 1st week of December.
  4. Follow up: remind folks of the deadline for their friends and family to use their gift cards

Winter is Coming: how to think (and act) to grow in Q4 →

Where it fits

There are diamonds in our own backyards. This is an awesome way to fill the pipeline for January and give your clients' friends and family an easy answer for what they're gonna do to tackle their resolutions this year. 

What actually gets you members:

The letter is from YOU and it's hand signed. That's step 1 for a reason. Signed = read. A printed flyer in an envelope is junk mail.

The card has a dollar value and a hard expiration date on it, in big type. Vague cards sit in a drawer.

Somebody follows up and makes deadlines clear, and the desk knows exactly what to do the second a card walks in (book them).

02 Decide First

5 decisions before you
print anything

Lock these in first. The letter, the card, the texts and the cheat sheet just plug them in.

1

Gift card value what the friend gets

Our rec

A free month is the strongest play you can make here assuming you're in a group training facility. Feels like a real gift, easy for the member to hand over. Make the dollar value of your gift card match what a month of training looks like so gift card shows real value. 

You can also plug your referral "bonuses" to clients in your letter to them RE: the gift card, e.g. "if your friend sticks around as a member afterwards, you'll get $100 off your next month so everybody wins! 

  • 2 weeks free is the trial version. Fine if your trial already converts and you want the card to feed your normal process. Gift card still needs to show real value here
  • $100 off month one is another safe default. Real money, and they're paying something on day one.
  • Whatever you pick, it's a noun phrase on the card and in the custom value: “$100 off month one,” “a free month,” “2 weeks free.” No “you” or “your.” The letter and the texts in the automations put the sentence around it.
2

Expiration date

Our rec

January 31 is the default. January is when fitness jumps back up the to-do list, so give them the whole month and then shut it. An end-of-January stop gives your follow-up something real to push against, and it keeps your reminders out of Christmas week.

  • Don't go past January 31. Past that the card is just a coupon.
  • Put the date on the card in big type and in the letter. Say it once, mean it. If you quietly extend it, the next deadline you send gets ignored.
3

Cards per member: 1 or 2

Our rec

2 cards per member. “Friends & family” is plural, couples are a thing, and the second card costs you almost nothing. The odds of every member attempting to bring someone in is almost zero, and worst case if you're overrun with referrals is to honor the cards but place folks on a waitlist to "claim" their prizes in February (assuming the referred prospect reached out originally before your deadline. 

4

The policy: new clients only

Clear is kind. Write the policy on the card AND in the letter, nicely, so nobody at the desk has to be the bad guy in January:

  • New clients only = folks who have never trained w/ you.
  • Past members? Your call. I'd say yes if they've been gone a year or more (best odds in the building). Decide now and tell the desk.
  • One card per referred person. Can't be combined w/ anything else. Not redeemable for cash (obviously, but say it).
  • Members can't use it on themselves. It's a gift for somebody else, and the letter says so.
5

Member reward what the member gets when their card gets used

Our rec

Your normal referral reward. A card coming back is a referral. Give $X/Get $X, same as any other month. Pay it the day the friend pays or apply it to their next month's membership.

  • No referral program yet? $50 off next month if avg membership is under $200/mo, $100 if above. Start one while you're at it (Referral Playbook linked in the checklist).
  • Say the reward in the letter so the member has a reason to actually hand the card over instead of meaning to.
03 The Letter

One page, from you,
signed by hand

Copy this into a Word doc or Canva on your letterhead, fill the brackets, print on decent paper. Edit it so it sounds like you, this is tough to templatize because frankly it should feel and be personal. Sign by hand. 

This prints as a plain one-pager if you'd rather not retype it. Fill the brackets in Word or Canva first, or by hand.

[GYM NAME + LOGO]
[DATE]

Hey [FIRST NAME],

Thanksgiving week always gets me thinking about the folks who make this place what it is, and that's you. Thank you for showing up. The early mornings, the “I almost didn't come today” days (those count double), all of it. Thank you for trusting us w/ something that actually matters.

It's been a great year here at [GYM NAME], from [STORIES, INSIDE JOKES, CLIENT SHOUTOUTS, UPGRADES, ETC.]

We couldn't have done it without you. 

Our team wanted to put something together as a tiny thank you and we came up with something we thought might make your holiday shopping a little easier and yes, your experience with us even more fun (is that even possible!?). 

Inside this envelope is a gift card for [GIFT CARD VALUE] here at [GYM NAME]. It's not for you (sorry!). It's for someone in your life who's been saying “I need to get back into something” for a while now. Your sister, your buddy from work, your partner, your mom. You know exactly who I mean :)

Here's how it works:

  • Hand them the card (or snap a pic and text it to them, that works too!)
  • They text us at [GYM NUMBER] to book their first session & claim their gift (you look like a hero - we won't tell them you didn't spend any money on it ;) 
  • The card is good through [EXPIRATION DATE], so don't let it sit in a drawer!

A couple of quick house rules so it's fair to everybody: the card is for new clients only, one card per person, and it can't be combined with any other offers or promotions.

Big bonus for you? If they join after their gift card is up, you get [MEMBER REWARD], same as any referral. You win, they win, we win.

We hope this makes your holiday shopping a little easier, and again, thank you from the bottom of our hearts for being an amazing part of the [GYM NAME] family. 

[SIGNOFF], 

[OWNER NAME, SIGNED BY HAND]
[GYM NAME]

Keep

  • The thank-you up top. It's a holiday letter w/ a gift in it. Members can smell a promo.
  • The policy line and the expiration date. Clear is kind.

Cut

  • Anything about your pricing, your January programs, or Black Friday. One ask per letter.
  • “Limited time” language. The date does that job.
04 The Gift Card

What goes on it

Attach a nice gift card w/ dollar value & expiration date clearly stated. That's the brief. We built the card for you. Copy the template, swap five things, send it to print.

Use the template

Gift of Fitness card template (Canva) →

  1. Open the link, click Edit template. It saves a copy to your Canva, the original stays put.
  2. Swap the five placeholders: your logo, [$ VALUE], [EXP DATE], your gym number, and any verbiage you like
  3. Leave the “From: ______” line on the back alone. That's how the desk knows who to thank.
  4. Download as PDF Print w/ crop marks and bleed.

Where to print it

  • Plastic cards (feels like a real gift card): the template is exact credit-card size (3.375 x 2.125 in), so the PDF goes to Plastic Printers, 4over4, or your local printer as-is. Roughly $0.25 to $1.50 a card depending on quantity; 4over4 starts around 25 to 50 cards.
  • Timing: plastic runs 2 to 3 weeks (we've seen folks get them faster locally). Order 2 per member + 10% spare.

If you'd rather build your own

Fine. Use the spec below so it still has everything the desk needs. Already have a buddy pass design? That works too: G.R.'s Quick n' Ugly buddy pass.

Size + stock

  • Envelopes: real gift card envelopes (the small ones w/ a slot), or a nice #10 if you're mailing. That's step 2 of the play: easy to hand to a friend.
05 How to Send

Mail it or hand it over

Send. Ideally around Thanksgiving to 1st week of December. In 2026 that's Mon Nov 23 to Fri Dec 4.

Hand out at the front desk

  • Envelopes behind the desk, sorted by name. Every member who walks in Nov 23 to Dec 4 gets theirs, by name, w/ one sentence: “Got something for you, open it when you get home.”
  • Free. And you get the moment, which is the whole reason the letter works.
  • Downside: you miss anybody who doesn't come in those two weeks, which is exactly the folks you want engaged in December. So...

Mail the rest

  • Fri Dec 4: whatever's still behind the desk goes in the mail. Hand-addressed if you can swing it, a real stamp (not metered). Lands the next week.
  • Roughly $1.50 to $2 per member all in (card, envelope, stamp). 150 members = under $300 for a play that lands in January.
  • Mailing everything is fine too if your desk/coaches are slammed. Then the envelope is doing the handshake, so make it a nice one.
Our rec

Hand out first, mail the rest Dec 4. And the signing: hand sign each one. If you've got a big team let them sign them. Do it at the staff meeting the week before, pizza optional. Fifteen minutes for 150 letters w/ four people signing.

The tracker

A sign-out sheet on the whiteboard (or a clipboard at the desk). Three columns: Member · # cards · Date handed over / mailed. When a card comes back, write the friend's name next to the member who sent it.

That's the tracker. Nothing else to maintain. In January you read down the sheet and you know who to pay.

07 When a Card Comes In

Front desk: book them
before they leave

A card walking in is a pre-sold referral. Book them on the spot. “Somebody will call you” is how you lose one.

  1. Take the card. Look at the From line. That's your member.
  2. Book the first session right then. Phone, calendar, done. Same as any intro. Tell them the [GIFT CARD VALUE] is locked in for them.
  3. Ask who sent them if the From line is blank: “Who do we get to thank for this?”
  4. Write the friend's name next to the member on the sign-out sheet. Put the member's name in the notes on the friend's profile too.
  5. Thank the member the same day. One text: “[FRIEND] just came in w/ your gift card! Love it. You'll get [MEMBER REWARD] the day they sign up.” Then pay it the day they join.
  6. Normal onboarding from there. The card value comes off at sign-up, nothing else changes.

The desk script

Anybody on staff

“Oh awesome, [MEMBER] gave you one of these! Love it. Let's get your first session on the books while you're here. What does your week look like, mornings or evenings?” [book it] “Perfect. You're in for [DAY + TIME], and your [GIFT CARD VALUE] is locked in. I'll text you the details right now. Can I grab your cell?”

Edge cases · decide now, tell the desk

Card expired last week?Honor it, once, and say so: “It's a little past the date but I'll take care of it.” Nobody gets turned away for a week.
Past member w/ a card?Whatever you decided in Decide First #4. Same answer from every coach.
Friend wants to start in February?Take the February start w/ the card value in writing and the start date on it. Write it on the sheet as a yes.
No From line, no idea who sent them?Still a new client. Book them, ask again at session one. Somebody will remember.
