01 The Rundown

Bring-a-friend week,
Halloween edition

Every active member can bring one person to train free all week. Run it the week before or the week of Halloween depending on where it falls. 2026 is perfect: the week leading up to Halloween. Bonus points for a little trunk-or-treat party to cap it all off.

“Boo” = anybody

Partner, friend, sibling, coworker, whoever. Keep it vague on purpose so single folks play too.

Internal only. No ads.

You're asking your best customers for a favor and making it easy and fun to say yes.

Limited capacity = built-in urgency

Capping guest spots per session lets you put real scarcity and urgency behind your registration deadlines.

The party is the excuse. Here's what actually gets you members:

You pick two numbers BEFORE announcing: the member reward and the boo offer.

Members sign up a NAME + a SESSION TIME (not just a name), so your 5:30am doesn't turn into a zoo.

Somebody sits down with every boo who comes in and walks them through the options before the referral offer expires.

PS: we've had clients CRUSH bring-a-friend weeks. Execute on this and you'll up the energy in your gym and drive sign-ups before you hit the holidays, all while making your members more likely to stay and pay because they have more friends training with them.

02 Decide First

4 decisions before you
touch the copy

Lock these in first. Everything else (emails, scripts, cheat sheet) just plugs them in.

1

Boo Day or Boo Week

  • Week = Mon–Sat. More bodies, more moving parts. The default if you want more opportunities and have staffed a bring-a-friend before.
  • Day = one Saturday session. Probably only the move if you've got a large group model with a ton of capacity, or you just want to do the absolute minimum.

The rest of this playbook is written for the week. Boo Day = same thing: skip the mid-week emails and say “Saturday” where it says “all week.”

2

Member reward what they get if their boo joins

Our rec

Your existing referral reward, with a bonus for boo week. Give $50 → Give $100 for the week. Give $100 → Give $200 (or $150) if you've got the margin. Doubling it for one window pours some gas on your referral fire before the holidays.

  • No referral program? $50 if avg membership is under $200/mo, $100 if above.
  • Alt that's worked: a $50–100 gift card to a local restaurant or coffee shop instead of account credit. Something they can't already buy from you, plus an excuse to ask that business to put your QR code by the register.
  • PAY IT THE DAY THE BOO JOINS. Doing it in front of people is great social proof and incentive, if you're comfortable making a little show of it.
Optional (we'd do it): the drawing

Every boo who shows up = one slip with the member's name in a jar at the desk. Draw one the Monday after boo week for something bigger than the standard reward (free month, big gift card, hoodie + a month).

No tracking, no automation. It's a jar. Announce it at every session and in the member group.

3

Boo offer what the guest gets if they join THIS week

Our rec

Same $ as the member reward off month one. Waive your start-up fee too if you charge one, but say that at the close, it doesn't go in the automations (the Boo Offer value is a dollar amount only). An alternative is legacy member rates, but $50–150 off for someone pre-sold should be an easy sale. Bonus points for throwing in some supplements or light merch.

  • Set an expiration date: either Saturday (the 31st in '26), or Wednesday if you want a few more days for the “gotta ask my spouse” folks.
  • If you're going with Wednesday, say it from day one. Don't quietly extend it after, or the next deadline you send gets ignored.
4

Halloween or not

If you're catching this at a different time of year, this is absolutely a valid play for other windows (e.g. Valentine's Day). The bring-a-friend concept still stands. We'd run 2–3 of these per year.

03 Capacity

Do this on paper
before you announce

The one way this play hurts you is overloading your best sessions. Paying members get a worse session and guests get a bad first impression, at the same time. So yes, a little planning and some simple math.

✕  Option A: Wing it

Hope you don't get that many people. Probably not the move, unless you've got huge capacity across all sessions or your member base isn't very engaged and you don't expect more than a handful of referrals.

Option B: Boo spots inside your normal capacity

“We typically have two spots open in X session and three in Y, so we'll have two boo spots for X and three for Y.” Boo sign-up is tracked per session time, and once they're full, they're full.

Option C: Extra boo spots on top We like this one

Leave normal registration as-is and build in additional spots per session based on your comfort level. If you normally run a 6:1 pod model, could you run a partner-workout format for a week, adjust programming slightly, and let everyone bring somebody? Probably. (Good problem to have.)

Bottom line: pick one
  • Add up your normal capacity per session and set a limit for each (e.g. 2 in session A, 3 in session B), or
  • Lock a flat number per session that either blocks normal registration or sits on top of it.

Then put that cap in your member messaging to drive scarcity and urgency. Create an artificial cap even if you have theoretically unlimited capacity in a large group model.

06 Talking Points

What your team says

Announce at sessions · every session, starting 2 weeks out

Coach, before warm-up

“Quick one before we get started. Week of [DATES] we're doing Bring Your Boo. Every one of you gets to bring somebody to train with you free all week: partner, friend, coworker, your mom, whoever. Just tell me their name and which session you're bringing them to so I can save the spot. We cap guests per session so it doesn't get crazy, so if you've got somebody, let us know ASAP. And if they end up joining, you get [MEMBER REWARD]. [Only if doing the drawing:] Plus every boo who shows up gets your name in the jar for [PRIZE] at the end of the week. So think about who you want to bring and let me know!”

Individual sign-up convos

When a member gives you a name, confirm the session time too: “Love it, which session are you bringing them to?” Then write both on the sign-up sheet in front of them.

The desk line

Every member, every day: “Who are you bringing to boo week?” Not “did you see the email.” Assume they didn't. Get the name AND the session time and write both down.

Get clear on the two numbers

Every coach says [MEMBER REWARD] and [BOO OFFER] the exact same way. Two people quoting different numbers is how you lose a sale you already had and have to awkwardly walk something back. Put both on the cheat sheet and have the team read them out loud at the staff meeting.

When a boo asks “how much is it?” on Tuesday

Candidly, the boo probably already knows what you charge, so pricing likely won't be a shock. But you do need to decide how your coaches handle it, and every coach handles it the same way.

Path 1: Give quick options on the floor

⚠️ Only do this if you've got a simple sign-up process with a couple of options, your coaches have time between sessions, and you have a proven history of converting people with a quick options conversation. If that's not you, use Path 2.
Coach

“Hey, thanks for asking! You loving it already?” [wait for the yes] “Awesome. So here are the options [show the 2–3 options]. Most folks get the best results with [BEST OPTION], and as a referral you get [BOO OFFER] off your first month… Which one makes the most sense to ya?”

Path 2: Book a quick call

If your coaches are terrified of asking for money, your sales process is complicated, or there's just no time between sessions:

Coach

“Oh hey, you loving it here already?” [wait for the yes] “That's awesome. Hey, I've got to get set up for my next session, and there are lots of options. I want to make sure you have time to look at what you need. Can I get you booked for a quick call with our owner later today? They'll take you through everything, and because [REFERRING MEMBER] brought you in, you get [BOO OFFER] off your first month. You got any time later today for a quick call?”

Book the boo on the calendar right then. A call is ideal here: they've already been in once or twice and they're excited, or they wouldn't be asking about price. Making them drive back in for what's likely a quick sign-up isn't worth the headache. Book ASAP. Same day, or even catching them on the drive home.

Day 1 for a boo

Greet by name at the door→ “You're here with [MEMBER], right?”→ Waiver→ 60-sec tour→ Intro to 2 members by name
  • The 60-second tour = the bathroom and where to put their stuff. The two things every new person is quietly worried about.
  • Introduce them to two members by name, not just the coach.
  • Mini consult + mini onboarding if you don't have much info on them: “So, are you currently working out or just getting started?” If they're active, get a feel for their routine and whether they need to be pushed or throttled back. If they're a rookie, use the “3–4 on a scale of 1 to 10” talk (walkthrough in the Circle course video) and follow up after the session.
  • 7-touch execution (in the Circle course): every member, boos included, gets a minimum of 7 individual interactions by name, every single session. Group cueing doesn't count. Coach your ass off.
On the way out

“Hey [NAME], great job! [Specific compliment, e.g. you killed it on the PICK-A-MOVEMENT]. Quick question, because we always touch base at the end of day one to customize how we're coaching everybody: how'd that feel intensity-wise? Too much? Too little? Or did we Goldilocks ya today? Because you're coming back Wednesday, right? We can make those adjustments based on how you're feeling.”

You're confirming visit 2 and giving yourself a chance to adjust to where they think they need to be to enjoy the experience.

07 The Close

Last day: it's a sales
conversation

Run it like one, even if it's abbreviated. We highly recommend whoever does your consults handles these, especially if your trainers are afraid of money conversations.

The major point

Get everyone who attended at least twice on the calendar ASAP. Same two paths as the price question above: a convo on the floor if you have time, or a booked call. Worst case, coaches catch them on their last session and book the call.

Possible scripting

Your normal trial verbiage works great too if you're comfortable and successful with your own process.

  1. Recap. “So you came in with [REFERRING MEMBER] Monday, you've been in 3 times, and [the thing they did, e.g. you pulled a deadlift Wednesday you didn't think you had in you]. How are you feeling about it?” Let them talk.
  2. Future pace. “If you kept this up, what changes for you?” Let THEM say it. Whatever they say here is what you say back to them at the close.
  3. The options. One boo offer, 2–3 memberships. Pull out your normal options sheet. “Here's the deal for folks who came in this week: [BOO OFFER] off your first month, good through [DEADLINE]. Most folks who train like you've been training this week go with [OPTION], but here's what we've got [walk the 2–3]. Oh, and [REFERRING MEMBER] gets [MEMBER REWARD] when you sign up too, so you both win. Which one makes the most sense to ya?”
  4. Then stop talking.
  5. Yes → phone, link, card. Book their first session as a member before they walk out.
  6. Objection → use the objection overcomes in the Sales Training Hub.
  7. No → be a cool person (they might come back later) and ask for feedback. If you didn't temp-check 'em earlier, this is a good spot to drop that in.
